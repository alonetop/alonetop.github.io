## Begin with a small problem

A seemingly simple layout issue once took an entire afternoon to solve. When it finally worked, I nearly closed the editor and moved on. Then I wondered how much time yesterday's version of me could have saved with today's explanation.

So I wrote a short note: the problem, the approaches I tried, and why the final one worked. No grand tutorial was required.

## Explaining is learning again

Trying to describe the process revealed a few things that worked by accident. I had to return to the documentation and build smaller experiments before I could explain them reliably.

The first person helped by a piece of writing is often the person writing it.

### A recording template

```markdown
## What happened

Describe the environment, expectation, and actual behavior.

## What I tried

Keep the meaningful attempts and why they failed.

## What I understand now

Explain the principle with a minimal working example.

## What remains uncertain

Leave an entrance for the next exploration.
```

## Learning does not need to become a performance

A useful record can be brief, and it can remain open-ended. What matters is being clear about its boundaries. Under which conditions does the conclusion hold? What has not yet been checked?

Honest limits make a note more dependable and make it easier for someone else to continue the investigation.

## Let knowledge move

Learning in public is not a demonstration of constant effort. It is a chance for experience to travel. Mark a small obstacle today; tomorrow you might find your own direction in someone else's notes.

A problem you just solved is a good enough place to begin.
