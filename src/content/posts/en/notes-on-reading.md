## Forgetting most of it

I forget most of what I read. That used to disappoint me: if I cannot remember a book, what was the point of reading it?

Looking back at books from years ago, I rarely remember individual chapters. I do remember a question that made me pause, or a way of explaining something that opened an unfamiliar angle.

Perhaps the influence of reading does not always take the form of memory.

## Keep less complete notes

I used to copy out many sentences. Without their context, those sentences soon became unfamiliar again. Now I try to add two small questions beside a quotation:

1. What does this bring to mind?
2. How does it connect to something I am dealing with?

Sometimes a whole book leaves me with just one question. That can be enough.

### Allow disagreement

Reading is not moving an author's opinions into your own head. It is closer to a conversation, with agreement, hesitation, and the occasional objection.

Writing “I am not sure about this yet” can be more honest than producing a neat conclusion. Uncertainty gives a book a way to remain in conversation with you.

## The next reading

Rereading is like returning to a familiar path. The path may be unchanged, but what catches your attention is different. A sentence you once passed over may become the place you most want to stop.

I no longer try to give every book a final verdict. A small trail is enough. A future version of me might follow it back.
